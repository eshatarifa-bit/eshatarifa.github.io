PhD direction

I am interested in the materials that connect the body to electronics. My undergraduate thesis on a gelatin–alginate hydrogel introduced me to designing and characterizing biomaterials. My published work on EEG-based emotion detection and Parkinson's detection gave me experience in biosignal processing. In my current electrochemical research, I am learning how to characterize sensing interfaces with cyclic voltammetry.

I want to bring these strands together in a PhD on soft, biocompatible materials for neural interfaces and brain–computer interfaces, and on how material properties affect the quality of the signals we record.

  • Hydrogel-based electrodes for EEG and other biosignals, where soft, stable skin contact affects signal quality.
  • Soft, biocompatible coatings for neural electrodes that aim to keep signals stable over time.
  • Photocurable and printable biomaterials that can be shaped into custom, functional devices.
